Echo.

A small Shazam-style engine that shows its work. Add songs, play a clip, and watch sound become fingerprints and find its match. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

Matches clips against the songs you add (plus 3 built-in demo tracks). No humming, no giant catalogue.

1. Library songs the engine knows

Added songs are kept in this browser (IndexedDB) as 8 kHz mono audio so they survive a reload. Delete them any time. MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, FLAC work if your browser can play them. Songs longer than 8 minutes are trimmed.

2. Make a query a short noisy clip

Noise slider: 30 dB is a clean clip, 0 dB means static as loud as the music, -10 dB means static louder than the music.

Try a phone recording of a song you added. Longer than 20 s is trimmed to the first 20 s.

Play a song you added from another device near the mic. Echo asks for microphone access only when you press the button, and does not store the recording.

3. What the engine saw

Step 1. Spectrogram of your clip, loudest points circled

Time runs left to right, pitch bottom to top (0 to 4 kHz). Bright means loud. Each circle is a peak: the loudest point in its neighbourhood. Static adds some peaks but the music's own peaks survive.

Step 2. Peaks become pairs, pairs become hashes

Each peak is paired with a few peaks just after it. A pair is stored as (pitch 1, pitch 2, gap in time), one number. Gaps, not clock times, so a clip can start anywhere. Lines show a sample of pairs.

Step 3. Every hash votes for a song and a position

Each hash is looked up in the library. A hit says "this pair occurs in song S at time T". If the clip really comes from S, all its hits agree on one offset (song time minus clip time). Wrong songs only collect scattered votes.

How it works

  1. Downsample to 8 kHz mono. Songs live below 4 kHz for fingerprinting purposes.
  2. Spectrogram: 1024-sample Hann windows, 256-sample hop, my own radix-2 FFT (no libraries).
  3. Peaks: keep points that are the loudest within ±10 pitch bins and ±8 frames and within 62 dB of the loudest point.
  4. Pairs: each peak pairs with up to 6 later peaks, 2 to 60 frames ahead. Hash = pitch1 (9 bits) + pitch2 (9 bits) + gap (6 bits).
  5. Index: a hash table from hash to (song, time).
  6. Match: histogram the offsets of all hits per song. A verdict needs at least 7 aligned votes and twice the runner-up's votes.

This is the classic landmark method (Wang, 2003). Open implementations exist, for example dejavu, Olaf and chirp. This one is a from-scratch learning build, not a new invention, and not a commercial service. It does not handle humming, big catalogues, big tempo or pitch changes, or heavily remixed audio. The 3 demo tracks are generated in code, so there are no copyright questions. Please only add songs you are allowed to use.
